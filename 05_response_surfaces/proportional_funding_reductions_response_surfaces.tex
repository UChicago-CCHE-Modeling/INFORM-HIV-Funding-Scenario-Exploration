\section{What changed in this version, and why}
In the previous version the stakeholder information (the ``elicited'' point and range) was drawn at fixed positions on the \emph{funding-loss} axes $\Delta$. During the team discussion on 7--8 October we re-read the survey questions (Table~\ref{tab:survey}) and concluded that this placed the stakeholder information in the wrong coordinate system:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item Questions 1 and 2 ask for the \textbf{reduction in PrEP use and in ART adherence} relative to current use. These are values of the coverage reduction $r$.
\item Questions 3 and 4 ask what share of current users could still obtain PrEP or ART by other means. These are the insulated shares $\gamma$.
\item Funding losses $\Delta$ were \textbf{not} elicited. They are derived: $\Delta_j=r_j/(1-\gamma_j)$.
\end{itemize}
Consequently the stakeholder point and range are fixed in $r$-space and are only \emph{re-expressed} on the $\Delta$ axes, where they move with the insulation assumption.

\textbf{Consequences.} (i) The incidence rate ratio (IRR) depends only on $r$. At a fixed stakeholder point the IRR is therefore the \emph{same} under every insulation assumption; the earlier statement that the elicited-point IRR ranges from 1.21 to 1.66 across insulation cases is withdrawn, as is the earlier fixed rectangle on the $\Delta$ axes and the earlier definition of P3. (ii) Insulation determines how large a funding loss is needed to produce a given $r$, and whether that $r$ is attainable at all, since $\Delta\le1$ requires $r_j\le1-\gamma_j$. (iii) The raw survey responses are not yet in hand. The point and range shown here are \textbf{provisional illustrative placeholders}, back-calculated from the earlier mock-up positions $\Delta=(0.50,0.35)$ at the median $\gamma$, and are not survey results. They will be replaced by respondent-level values; because respondents differ in both $r$ and $\gamma$, each respondent's pair must satisfy $r_j\le1-\gamma_j$, and combining one respondent's $r$ with another's $\gamma$ can manufacture an inconsistent case.

\section{Definitions and cases}
Let $r_{PrEP}$ be the proportional reduction in PrEP use and $r_{VS}$ the proportional reduction in viral suppression (the ART channel). Let $\Delta_{prevention}$ be the proportional reduction in prevention funding and $\Delta_{Medicaid}$ the proportional loss of Medicaid coverage among people in HIV care (mapped one-to-one to the ART channel). With $\gamma_j$ the insulated share of current users,
\[
 r_j=\Delta_j(1-\gamma_j),\qquad \Delta_j=\frac{r_j}{1-\gamma_j}.
\]
The surrogate gives the IRR as a function of $(r_{PrEP},r_{VS})$ only, over $[0,0.75]^2$. The insulation share does not enter the model; it only translates between $r$ and $\Delta$. We use the survey medians ($\gamma_{PrEP}=0.40$, $\gamma_{ART}=0.75$) and relative $\pm25\%$ variations as a deliberate sensitivity exercise (not survey bounds or confidence intervals). Because $\Delta\le1$, the largest reachable $r_j$ is $1-\gamma_j$.

\textbf{Scenarios (provisional definitions, in $r$-space).} P1: treatment component only, $(0,\,r^*_{VS})$. P2: prevention component only, $(r^*_{PrEP},\,0)$. P3: both, $(r^*_{PrEP},\,r^*_{VS})$, which coincides with the provisional illustrative point. The placeholder values are $r^*_{PrEP}=0.30$ and $r^*_{VS}=0.0875$, with a placeholder range $r_{PrEP}\in[0.15,0.48]$, $r_{VS}\in[0.03,0.20]$. These definitions replace the earlier ones (P3 was previously ``prevention eliminated plus Medicaid change'').

\textbf{Outcome.} All results are the IRR in the \textbf{final year} (post-intervention year 10), not an average over the trajectory. Intervals are 95\% model-uncertainty intervals over 100 calibration checkpoints $\times$ 50 surrogate draws, with common random numbers.

\subsection*{Reading the results}
At the provisional illustrative point, the final-year IRR is 1.43 (1.28--1.61). The treatment-only scenario P1 gives 1.22 (1.15--1.31) and the prevention-only scenario P2 gives 1.19 (1.05--1.36). The excess IRRs of P1 (0.22) and P2 (0.19) sum to 0.41, versus 0.43 for P3: the two components are close to additive, with a slight excess; intervals overlap widely. All values are conditional on the provisional placeholders and the three insulation cases; the intervals reflect model and surrogate uncertainty only.

The insulation assumption does not change these health outcomes at a fixed $r$. It changes the funding interpretation. For the same point, the implied losses are $(\Delta_{prevention},\Delta_{Medicaid})=(0.43,0.20)$ under lower insulation, $(0.50,0.35)$ at the survey medians, and $(0.60,1.40)$ under higher insulation, the last of which is not attainable. Stated differently, if the stakeholders' expected reductions are as large as the placeholder values, then high insulation of ART is inconsistent with them: with 94\% of ART users insulated, even complete loss of Medicaid coverage could reduce viral suppression by at most 6\%.

\subsection{How we arrived at this set of plots}
\textbf{From dollars to proportions.} The first analysis expressed cuts in dollars. Interpreting dollar changes requires assumptions about which funding streams reach the modeled population, which are hard to defend. The team moved to proportional reductions without dollar amounts or iso-dollar lines; a dollar translation can return in the interactive tool.

\textbf{Two coordinate systems.} Anna's example figures separate a funding view ($\Delta$ axes with the resulting $r$ on secondary axes) from a pure coverage view with absolute percentage-point axes. The percentage-point axes matter because the same proportional reduction removes much more coverage from viral suppression (about 61\%) than from PrEP (about 23\%): PrEP cannot lose more than its baseline, roughly 17 points at $r_{PrEP}=0.75$.

\textbf{Interaction plots.} Multi-line interaction plots were judged hard to read and the interaction small; a table of treatment-only, prevention-only and combined reductions was preferred. P1, P2 and P3 now provide exactly this comparison.

\textbf{Final year, not the trajectory mean.} The paper reports the final year, since IRRs change over time. In rebuilding the figures we found that the earlier ``tick 10'' is surrogate row 10 (the ninth intervention year); the final year is row 11 (simulation year 32, post-intervention year 10; row 6 is year 5). Earlier horizon-mean and row-10 results should not be quoted as final-year values.

\textbf{Which $\gamma$.} Options discussed: separate exemplar cases (shows how much the assumption matters); integrating over a $\gamma$ distribution (hides the individual effect); or a compromise, exemplar cases in the paper and both user-chosen insulation and optional integration in the app. The compromise was favored. With only five survey responses there is no defensible interval, so we use relative $\pm25\%$ around the medians. The survey minimums (0.14 PrEP, 0.15 ART) would require $r$ up to about 0.86, beyond the range on which the surrogate was trained ($r\le0.75$), so they are not shown.

\textbf{The correction in this version.} On 7--8 October the team re-read the elicitation questions and recognized that the stakeholder values are reductions in use ($r$) and insulated shares ($\gamma$), not funding losses. Jonathan and Anna independently reached this reading, and it is consistent with the question wording (Table~\ref{tab:survey}). The earlier $\Delta$-space placement of the stakeholder box was a placeholder from the mock-up; the figures were rebuilt in $r$-space. The visual comparison across insulation cases is less dramatic as a result (the IRR inside the box no longer changes), but it is now correct, and the attainability of the points under each case is itself informative.

\textbf{Communication.} Figures with two sets of axes are dense for a public-health readership (the target journal is Lancet HIV). The team favored keeping the figures but making captions self-contained, with a worked reading and the definitions of P1--P3, and placing the more complex funding-mapping figure after a simple $r$-space figure (not as the first figure in the paper). A $2\times2$ composite (one $r$-space panel and the median $\Delta$ panel, or all) remains an option.

\textbf{Baselines.} The meeting discussed a PrEP baseline of about 23\% and an ART/viral-suppression baseline of 61\%. With the saved surrogate these baselines do not change the IRR at a given $r$; they only scale the percentage-point labels. The source of the 23\% is not settled: saved calibration data suggest a PrEP input of about 0.20 and realized pre-intervention use of about 19\%.

\subsection{Still to be done or confirmed}

\item \textbf{Obtain the raw survey responses} ($r_{PrEP}$, $r_{VS}$, $\gamma_{PrEP}$, $\gamma_{ART}$ per respondent) and replace the placeholders. Check $r_j\le1-\gamma_j$ for each respondent and report implied $\Delta$ and attainability per respondent, rather than pairing separate medians.
\item Ask AFC to confirm the expected prevention-funding reduction and the proportion of Black sexual minority men expected to lose Medicaid coverage; consider sending each respondent a figure based on their own answers to check consistency (the survey was anonymous, so this would go through AFC).
\item Confirm that ``ART adherence'' (item 2) may stand in for viral suppression.
\item Confirm the origin of the 23\% PrEP baseline, or drop the percentage-point axes from the main figure.
\item Confirm the $\pm25\%$ insulation cases or supply survey-based bounds; consider mixed $(\gamma_{PrEP},\gamma_{ART})$ combinations.
\item Decide whether to extend the surrogate beyond $r=0.75$.
\item Update the paper text to the final-year definition (row 11) and to the $r$-space interpretation.
\item New simulation runs: hysteresis and recovery time after a period of reduced funding (checkpoint after the reduction, restore coverage, measure time to return to baseline incidence; possible start-up costs raise the funding needed per unit of $r$). App: user-selectable insulation, optional integration over $\gamma$, dollar translation.
\end{itemize}
